Six questions, and what answers them

NIST's AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001 and the EU AI Act don't name tools. But once a review gets down to the running system, these are the questions that come up, and these are the parts that answer them.

THEMEWHAT YOU GET ASKEDWHAT ANSWERS IT
01

Data residency

"Where do the prompts go, and who else sees them?"
  • circuit-ollama models on your host, API on 127.0.0.1 TESTED
  • circuit-openwebui telemetry and sharing off; no OpenAI connection unless you add one TESTED
02

Access and budgets

"Who can use which model, and how much can they spend?"
  • circuit-litellm a key per team or person, model lists, dollar budgets; wrong model gets 403 TESTED
  • circuit-openwebui sign-up shut, admin made before the port opens, models granted per user TESTED
03

Audit trail

"Who asked, which model answered, and what did it cost?"
  • circuit-litellm a spend-log row per request, prompts left out by default TESTED
  • circuit-langfuse every prompt, answer, cost and latency, as traces on your host TESTED
04

Personal data

"What stops a customer's details reaching a model?"
  • circuit-presidio finds and masks names, emails, cards; best effort, not a guarantee TESTED
  • LiteLLM guardrail Presidio on every request through the gateway COMING
05

Change evidence

"What changed, when, and is it still true today?"
  • conn3x plan every change as a diff, cloud and hosts in one
  • conn3x drift exits 2 when someone changed it by hand
  • pins images by tag and digest; state encryption from OpenTofu
06

Host hardening

"Is the machine underneath locked down?"
  • baseline · hardening · firewall key-only SSH, ufw, nothing open but SSH TESTED
  • circuit-caddy TLS in front, so services stay on loopback
TESTEDthe circuit's README says how COMINGnot shipped yet

Helps you meet it

None of this makes a system compliant, and no circuit is certified against any framework. It gives you parts that do what they say, and a record of what you ran.
